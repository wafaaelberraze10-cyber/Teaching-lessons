# Project-Based Learning (PBL) in ELA

[Home](../../README.md) / [Teaching Guides](lesson-planning.md) / Project-Based Learning

Project-Based Learning (PBL) is a teaching method where students learn by actively engaging in real-world, personally meaningful projects. This guide outlines the core elements of ELA projects and how to design them.

## Core Elements of High-Quality PBL

According to Gold Standard PBL (Buck Institute for Education):

* **Challenging Problem or Question:** The project is framed by a meaningful, open-ended question (e.g., *"How can we use literature to address modern social isolation?"*).
* **Sustained Inquiry:** Students engage in an extended process of asking questions, finding resources, and applying information.
* **Authenticity:** The project features real-world context, tasks, and impact.
* **Student Voice & Choice:** Students make decisions about the project products and how they work.
* **Reflection & Revision:** Students and teachers reflect on learning and give/receive peer feedback to improve products.
* **Public Product:** Students make their work public by presenting it to an audience beyond their classroom (e.g., parents, local experts).

## Designing ELA Projects with AI
AI can assist in brainstorming driving questions, structuring milestones, and designing authentic project briefs:

* **Driving Question Brainstormer:** Prompting the AI to connect CCSS standards to current local community challenges.
* **Milestone Pacing Builder:** Using AI to break down a 4-week project into daily milestones (e.g., research, drafting, editing, slide formatting, public rehearsals).

---

## Example PBL Blueprint

* **Driving Question:** *How can we write a digital guide to help international students adjust to our local community?*
* **Target Standards:** CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.9-10.4 (writing clear and coherent text), CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.9-10.7 (conducting research projects).
* **Final Product:** A published Google Site or Notion page directory.
* **Public Audience:** Local community center, school counselors, and incoming students.
