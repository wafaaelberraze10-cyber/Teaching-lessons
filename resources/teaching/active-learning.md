## Core Collaborative Structures

### 1. The Jigsaw Method
* **What it is:** Students are split into "Home Groups" and then reform into "Expert Groups" to study a specific section of a text before returning to teach their home group.
* **Why it matters:** Develops peer-teaching skills, accountability, and comprehension.

### 2. Socratic Seminar / Fishbowl Debate
* **What it is:** An inner circle of students discusses a text's themes while an outer circle observes and tracks speaking participation or arguments.
* **Why it matters:** Promotes speaking, listening, and evidence-backed argumentation.

### 3. Reciprocal Teaching
* **What it is:** Students lead small group reading sessions by rotating four roles: Predictor, Questioner, Clarifier, and Summarizer.

## AI as an Active Learning Partner

```
Student ◄───► AI Chatbot (Debate Opponent: "Act as a critic of my argument")
                     │
                     ▼
Teacher (Moderator/Evaluator)
```

Teachers can use AI to facilitate active, independent brainstorming or debating:

* **Socratic Opponent:** Students prompt ChatGPT or Claude to act as a devil's advocate. For example, *"I will argue that Hamlet was truly insane. You act as a critic who believes he was faking, and challenge my assertions with evidence."*
* **Interviewing Characters:** Students prompt the AI to stay in character (e.g., *"Act as Macbeth. I will ask you questions about your motivations"*), forcing students to formulate questions based on text facts.

## Implementation Guidelines
1. **Set Time Limits:** Active learning can devolve into off-task talk if intervals are too long. Keep intervals short (e.g., 3-5 minutes of discussion).
2. **Assign Individual Roles:** Ensure every student in a group has a specific duty (e.g., Writer, Timekeeper, Presenter).
3. **Use Discussion Scaffolding:** Display sentence stems (e.g., *"I agree with your point, but have you considered..."*) on the board.
