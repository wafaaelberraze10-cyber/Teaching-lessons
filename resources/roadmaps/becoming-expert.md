## Milestones by Phase

### Phase 1: The Master Teacher (Years 3–5)
* **Focus:** Deepen classroom practice, student outcomes, and peer mentoring.
* **Key Actions:**
  - Mentor pre-service or first-year teachers.
  - Pursue advanced credentials (e.g., National Board Certification in the US, or DELTA for ESL).
  - Lead department PLC (Professional Learning Community) groups.

### Phase 2: The EdTech & AI Specialist (Years 5–8)
* **Focus:** Leading school-wide technology and artificial intelligence initiatives.
* **Key Actions:**
  - Obtain Google Certified Coach or Microsoft Certified Educator credentials.
  - Draft school-wide AI guidelines and digital citizenship protocols.
  - Present at national EdTech conferences (e.g., ISTE, Bett).

### Phase 3: The Educational Leader (Years 8+)
* **Focus:** Shaping policy, district curricula, or campus administration.
* **Key Actions:**
  - Complete an M.Ed or Ph.D. in Educational Leadership, Curriculum and Instruction, or Instructional Design.
  - Apply for Assistant Principal, Director of Curriculum, or Academic Director positions.

## Transitioning to Instructional Design
Instructional Design (ID) is a rapidly growing field for former teachers. IDs build curricula and training systems for school districts, universities, or corporate settings:

* **Core Skills to Build:**
  - Mastery of ADDIE (Analyze, Design, Develop, Implement, Evaluate) and SAM models.
  - Competency in authoring tools (Articulate Storyline, Adobe Captivate).
  - High proficiency in LMS (Learning Management System) administration.
