## Holistic vs. Analytic vs. Single-Point Rubrics

* **Holistic Rubrics:** Provide a single, overall score based on an integrated assessment of student performance. (Best for quick, low-stakes grading).
* **Analytic Rubrics:** Break down the components of an assignment into distinct criteria (e.g., Thesis, Evidence, Syntax, Citations) with performance levels for each. (Best for detailed feedback on formal essays).
* **Single-Point Rubrics:** List only the target "proficient" standards in a center column, with blank spaces on the left for "Areas of Improvement" and on the right for "Evidence of Exceeding Standard". (Best for student-focused, formative revision).

## Analytic Rubric Structure

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Thesis Statement** | Clear, arguable, and highly complex; maps out essay arguments. | Clear and arguable; states the essay's direction. | Vague or simple; restates the prompt without analysis. | Missing or does not address the topic. |
| **Evidence & Analysis** | Integrates high-quality quotes seamlessly; analysis goes beyond surface meaning. | Quotes support claims; analysis explains connection to thesis. | Quotes are present but poorly integrated or explain little. | Missing quotes or analysis is irrelevant. |

## AI Rubric Generation Prompt

* **Prompt:**
  ```text
  Act as an expert writing instructor. Generate a 4-point analytic rubric for a Grade 11 argumentative essay focusing on literary analysis. The rubric must evaluate four criteria:
  1. Thesis & Focus
  2. Textual Evidence Integration
  3. Structural Organization & Transitions
  4. Grammar & Style Mechanics
  Ensure the descriptors for each level (4, 3, 2, 1) are concrete, measurable, and do not use vague terms like "satisfactory" or "excellent" without defining them.
  ```
