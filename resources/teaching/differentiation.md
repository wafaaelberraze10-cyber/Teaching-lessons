## Tomlinson's Differentiation Model

Teachers can differentiate across four core areas based on student readiness, interest, or learning profile:

1. **Content (What they learn):** Offering reading materials at different Lexile/reading levels or providing supplementary visual aids.
2. **Process (How they learn):** Giving students different graphic organizers, varying the pace of learning, or utilizing reading partners.
3. **Product (How they demonstrate mastery):** Allowing choices for final projects (e.g., writing an essay, delivering a speech, creating a digital comic).
4. **Learning Environment (Where they learn):** Designing quiet workspaces, organizing collaborative groups, or offering digital text-to-speech tools.

## Practical Differentiation Tactics

| Area | Strategy | AI Implementation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Content** | Leveling reading passages | Use **Diffit** or **ChatGPT** to rewrite an article to grade levels 5, 8, and 11. |
| **Process** | Scaffolded vocabulary | Use **QuillBot** or **Gemini** to compile a list of definitions, synonyms, and translation glossaries. |
| **Product** | Assessment choice | Prompt **Claude** to generate three alternative project prompts of equal academic rigor. |

## AI Differentiation Workflows

### 1. Generating Vocabulary Scaffolding Glossaries
* **Prompt:**
  ```text
  Act as a bilingual support teacher. Take the following text passage and create a scaffolded vocabulary glossary for CEFR A2/B1 level English learners. For each of the 5 most difficult words, provide:
  1. The word as used in context.
  2. A simplified definition.
  3. A translation in Spanish.
  4. An example sentence using the word.
  
  Text: [Insert Text Here]
  ```
