## Specialized Classroom AI Tools

### Quiz & Assessment Generators

#### 1. Quizizz AI
* **Why it matters:** Generates gamified quizzes instantly from uploaded PDFs, YouTube videos, or web links.
* **Who it's for:** ELA teachers looking to run active retrieval practice sessions.
* **Pros:** Highly engaging game modes (teleport, power-ups); imports directly into Google Classroom.
* **Cons:** Premium game formats and analytics require a paid school license.
* **Cost:** Free basic tier; Individual premium is $96/year.
* **Link:** [https://quizizz.com](https://quizizz.com)

#### 2. Kahoot! AI Question Generator
* **Why it matters:** Creates multiple-choice questions from simple prompts to seed class games.
* **Who it's for:** Teachers launching vocabulary review sessions.
* **Link:** [https://kahoot.com](https://kahoot.com)

#### 3. Conker
* **Why it matters:** AI-specific quiz builder that outputs direct printable tests aligned to standard educational benchmarks.
* **Link:** [https://www.conker.ai](https://www.conker.ai)

---

### Flashcard & Vocabulary Builders

#### 1. Quizlet AI
* **Why it matters:** Uses AI to generate flashcards, definitions, and practice tests. Includes "Q-Chat", an interactive AI tutor.
* **Who it's for:** ESL/EFL students studying high-frequency vocabulary.
* **Pros:** Clean interface; automated study schedules.
* **Cons:** Free tier features are heavily restricted by advertisements.
* **Cost:** Free basic version; Quizlet Plus is $35.99/year.
* **Link:** [https://quizlet.com](https://quizlet.com)

#### 2. Anki
* **Why it matters:** Open-source spaced repetition flashcard system. Teachers use AI to generate formatted text lists to import into Anki for permanent vocabulary acquisition.
* **Link:** [https://apps.ankiweb.net](https://apps.ankiweb.net)

---

### Reading & Text Differentiation Tools

#### 1. Diffit
* **Why it matters:** Instantly adapts any article, YouTube video, or text passage to any grade level or reading ability. It generates matching comprehension questions, vocabulary lists, and classroom activities.
* **Who it's for:** Teachers with mixed-ability classes or ESL students.
* **Pros:** Export options to Google Docs, Slides, and Forms.
* **Cons:** Advanced export designs require a premium subscription.
* **Cost:** Free basic access; school site licensing.
* **Link:** [https://beta.diffit.me](https://beta.diffit.me)

#### 2. ReadTheory
* **Why it matters:** Adaptive reading comprehension platform that uses AI to adjust text passage difficulty based on student answers.
* **Link:** [https://readtheory.org](https://readtheory.org)

### Speaking & Pronunciation Practice

#### 1. ELSA Speak
* **Why it matters:** AI speech coach that analyzes pronunciation, intonation, and fluency down to the phoneme level, offering visual feedback on syllable stress and vocal mechanics.
* **Who it's for:** Intermediate/advanced ESL/EFL students working on speaking confidence.
* **Pros:** Highly accurate pronunciation diagnostics; personalized daily training routines.
* **Cons:** Requires a smartphone/tablet (not optimized for standard web browsers).
* **Cost:** Free basic diagnostics; Pro starts at $11.99/month.
* **Link:** [https://elsaspeak.com](https://elsaspeak.com)

---

### AI Content Detectors

#### 1. GPTZero
* **Why it matters:** Scans texts to detect patterns typical of generative AI.
* **Who it's for:** Teachers evaluating academic integrity.
* **Pros:** Provides sentence-level analyses and highlights mixed AI/human writing.
* **Cons:** **High false-positive rate** (especially for non-native English speakers who write in formal, structured patterns). AI detectors should never be the sole basis for disciplinary action.
* **Cost:** Free basic scanner; Professional tiers available.
* **Link:** [https://gptzero.me](https://gptzero.me)

#### 2. Copyleaks
* **Why it matters:** Enterprise-grade detector with built-in plagiarism checking.
* **Link:** [https://copyleaks.com](https://copyleaks.com)

## Ethical AI Guidelines for Schools
1. **Prioritize Data Privacy:** Never input student names, IDs, grades, or personal details into public, non-enterprise AI systems.
2. **Encourage transparency:** Establish clear school guidelines defining what constitutes *assistive* AI use (e.g., brainstorming) vs. *inauthentic* AI use (e.g., generating entire essays).
3. **Acknowledge Bias:** Generative models are trained on internet data and can carry cultural biases. Discuss these biases openly with students as part of digital literacy lessons.
