## Actionable Milestones

### Level 1: The Curious Explorer (Basic Literacy)
* **Objective:** Understand how Large Language Models work, their primary limitations (hallucinations, data privacy), and basic prompt patterns.
* **Competencies to Build:**
  - Learn basic prompt structures (role, context, instruction).
  - Use ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude for simple brainstorming (e.g., generating class discussion questions).
  - Complete the free *OpenAI for K-12 Educators* course or *Google AI Essentials*.

### Level 2: The Integrated Co-Pilot (Applied Mastery)
* **Objective:** Embed AI workflows into daily curriculum design, assessment creation, and text differentiation.
* **Competencies to Build:**
  - Master advanced prompt patterns (few-shot prompting, chain-of-thought).
  - Differentiate text materials using tools like **Diffit** or custom prompts.
  - Create and grade essays using detailed analytic rubrics linked to AI feedback (using platforms like **Edusage AI**).
  - Set up specialized tools like **NotebookLM** for class reference collections.

### Level 3: The Innovation Leader (Architect Level)
* **Objective:** Lead school-wide professional development, design institutional AI policies, and automate administrative tasks using scripts.
* **Competencies to Build:**
  - Conduct training workshops for colleagues.
  - Write custom automation workflows using Google Apps Script or Python.
  - Consult on school board AI security, data protection, and ethical integration policies.

## Future Skills for AI-Augmented ELA Teaching

### 1. Computational Thinking in CALL (CTCALL)
Developing students' algorithmic thinking and debugging skills to deconstruct complex language tasks, structure prompts, and critically evaluate AI-generated outputs.

### 2. Mitigating Metacognitive Laziness (The Cognitive Mirror)
Mastering instructional designs where AI acts as a "teachable novice" to trigger the Protégé Effect. This forces students to explain concepts to the AI, externalizing their thought processes rather than passively copying generated text.

### 3. Data Literacy and Ethical AI
Interpreting complex classroom dashboard data for targeted pedagogical interventions. Teachers must teach students proper attribution, algorithmic plagiarism avoidance, and critical awareness of training data bias.

### 4. Universal Design for Learning (UDL)
Leveraging multimodal AI tools (text-to-speech, synthetic audio, graphic generators) to ensure school curricula are natively accessible to neurodivergent, visually/hearing impaired, and diverse linguistic profiles.

## AI Policy Framework for Classrooms
* **Ethics First:** Student privacy is paramount. Ensure no personally identifiable student data is submitted to public AI models.
* **Human-in-the-Loop:** AI outputs are starting drafts. Teachers must audit every AI-generated sheet, test, or email for accuracy and pedagogical suitability.
