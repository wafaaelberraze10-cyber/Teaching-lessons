## Academic Success & Research Skills
* **Deep Reading Habits:** As a future English teacher, read widely across genres (classical, contemporary, YA literature, non-fiction) and language acquisition journals.
* **Academic Writing:** Master citation styles (MLA and APA) early. Understand research methodologies to leverage evidence-based practices in your classroom.
* **AI Literacy:** Do not avoid AI, but do not rely on it. Use AI to brainstorm, but write your own essays. Familiarity with AI workflows makes you highly competitive in the job market.

## Preparing for Student Teaching & Practicum
* **Micro-Teaching:** Take every opportunity in college classes to practice teaching 10-minute mini-lessons to your peers. Focus on classroom presence, clear instructions, and transitions.
* **Observation Logs:** When observing classrooms, do not just watch the students. Watch the teacher's movements, pacing, voice modulation, and correction strategies.
* **Professional Etiquette:** Arrive early, dress professionally, and respect the cooperating teacher's classroom culture.

## Building a Professional Portfolio

```
Your Portfolio ──► [Teaching Philosophy] + [Sample Lesson Plans] + [Student Work Exemplars]
```

Build a digital portfolio using free hosting tools (e.g., GitHub Pages, Notion, or Canva Sites):

* **GitHub for Teachers:** Host your lesson plans and teaching templates in a public repository to show collaboration and digital organization skills.
* **LinkedIn Profile:** Customize your headline (e.g., *"Pre-Service ELA Teacher \| B.Ed Student at [University]"*), connect with local educators, and share classroom lessons you design.
* **Artifacts to Include:**
  - Standard-aligned unit outlines.
  - Video recordings of demo lessons (with student faces blurred/consent acquired).
  - Sample worksheets and grading rubrics.

## Mastering the Job Interview & Demo Lesson
1. **The Resume:** Highlight practicum classroom hours, age groups taught, standards used, and technology integrations.
2. **Interview Questions:** Be ready for scenario questions (e.g., *"How do you handle a student who refuses to work?"*, *"How do you differentiate for ESL students?"*).
3. **The Demo Lesson:**
   - Keep it active: minimize lecturing; maximize student reading/writing/speaking time.
   - Use a simple hook and check for understanding frequently.
   - End with a concrete exit ticket.

## Drafting Your Teaching Philosophy
Your teaching philosophy is a 1-page statement explaining *why* you teach, *how* you teach, and *how* you assess.

### Suggested Structure:
1. **The Hook:** A personal belief or educational quote about language and literacy.
2. **Pedagogical Approach:** Explain your method (e.g., *Student-centered, communicative language teaching, inquiry-based literature studies*).
3. **Assessment:** How you measure growth (formative assessment, growth mindset grading).
4. **Conclusion:** Your vision for student empowerment in a digital world.
