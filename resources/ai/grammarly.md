## Overview
Grammarly is a comprehensive writing assistant that uses artificial intelligence to review spelling, grammar, punctuation, clarity, engagement, and delivery in written texts. With its educational licenses and real-time feedback, it serves as an inline editing coach that guides students to refine their mechanics.

---

## Use Cases in the English Classroom
* **Formative Writing Feedback:** Providing students with real-time grammar and stylistic feedback while they draft essays.
* **Self-Editing Skills:** Teaching students how to evaluate writing suggestions, deciding which changes improve clarity and which alter their voice.
* **Plagiarism Detection:** Scanning student essays against billions of web pages to ensure academic integrity.
* **Citation Assistance:** Generating correct MLA, APA, or Chicago style citations from online resources.

---

## Strengths & Weaknesses
### Strengths
* High integration: runs seamlessly inside Chrome, Google Docs, Microsoft Word, and email clients.
* Explains the "Why": provides grammatical explanations for each suggestion, turning errors into learning moments.
* Customizable tone goals (e.g., formal, informative, creative, analytical).

### Weaknesses
* Students can develop an over-reliance on the tool, clicking "Accept" blindly without understanding the error.
* Stylistic suggestions can sometimes wash out unique or culturally diverse voices in creative writing.

## Best Practices for Teachers
1. **Disable Auto-Correct During Drafts:** Encourage students to draft without Grammarly enabled. Turn it on during the "Edit" phase to prevent the tool from disrupting creative flow.
2. **Review the Grammatical Rationale:** Require students to write down three grammar corrections they made using Grammarly, explaining the rule behind each mistake in a reflection log.
3. **Set Tone Intentions:** Have students configure Grammarly's target audience and tone goals before starting their essays to align the AI's suggestions with their assignment goals.

## Example Editing Workflows

### 1. Peer-Editing Companion Activity
* **Goal:** Practice critical editing skills using Grammarly's explanations.
* **Instructions for Students:**
  1. Swap drafts with a peer.
  2. Open the draft in Google Docs with the Grammarly extension enabled.
  3. For every red correction line:
     - Read the AI's explanation.
     - Discuss with your partner whether the correction is necessary or if the original phrasing is better for the author's voice.
     - Document your decision.

## Classroom Examples
* **Weekly Grammar Spotlight:** Take a sentence that Grammarly flagged in a common class document. Display it on the board. Ask students to identify the error, propose a fix, and then click Grammarly's explanation cards to see if their explanation matches the AI's grammatical rules.
