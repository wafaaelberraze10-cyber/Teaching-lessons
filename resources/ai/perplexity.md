## Overview
Perplexity AI is a conversational search engine designed to answer questions by retrieving web pages, documents, and databases in real time. It presents structured answers with clear, clickable inline citations, making it an invaluable tool for student research, fact-checking, and lesson planning.

---

## Use Cases in the English Classroom
* **Fact-Checking & Author Backgrounds:** Gathering accurate, citation-backed historical context on authors, literary periods, or specific texts.
* **Finding Educational Material:** Searching for high-quality, free-to-use reading passages, news articles, or public domain short stories.
* **Teaching Information Literacy:** Showing students how to evaluate source quality by tracing AI assertions back to the cited websites.
* **Academic Review:** Summarizing recent pedagogical studies regarding reading comprehension or language acquisition.

---

## Strengths & Weaknesses
### Strengths
* High transparency: every assertion has an accompanying inline footnote citation linking to the source.
* Search "Focus" settings let you target specific areas (e.g., *Academic* for peer-reviewed studies, *Writing* for text-only without web search, *YouTube* for video resources).
* Dramatically reduces AI hallucinations by basing answers on live search results.

### Weaknesses
* Not optimized for long-form creative writing tasks or complex conversational brainstorming.
* If search results are inaccurate or low-quality, the compiled response will reflect those biases.

## Best Practices for Teachers
1. **Utilize "Focus" Modes:** Use the **Academic** focus to find pedagogical papers or literary analysis. Use the **Writing** focus to generate texts without pulling in irrelevant web search links.
2. **Teach the 3-Step Verification:** Show students how to:
   1. Read the AI summary.
   2. Click the cited link to view the original website.
   3. Verify if the source is reputable (e.g., a university or official library).
3. **Save Collections:** Use Perplexity "Collections" to group your research threads for specific lesson units, sharing them with colleagues or students.

## Example Prompts

### 1. Author Biography with Academic Sources
* **Purpose:** Create a biographical context sheet for a literature class.
* **Inputs:** Author (e.g., Langston Hughes), Target Level (Grade 9).
* **Prompt:**
  ```text
  Search Focus: Academic
  Provide a biography of Langston Hughes suitable for a Grade 9 English class. Highlight his role in the Harlem Renaissance and list three of his major works. Ensure all statements are cited from academic or primary databases (.edu or library sources).
  ```
* **Expected Output:** A 3-paragraph cited biography with active link references to academic websites.
* **Customization Tips:** Request a secondary section outlining the historical backdrop of the Great Migration.

## Classroom Examples
* **Research Literacy Exercise:** Display a Perplexity search on the board. Ask it: *"What is the main theme of 'To Kill a Mockingbird'?"* Then, have students audit the sources cited by Perplexity. Are they blogs, study guides, or university journals? Teach them how to replace low-quality sources with scholarly ones.
